You graduated from school, got your first job, and after a couple months finally have some money saved up to invest.  Your goal is to grow your investments as large as possible before you retire.  Or even better become financially independent.

You go and meet with two investment advisors to help you with your investments and reach your retirement goal.  Both advisors are friendly, professional, and talk lots about compound interest.

The only difference is how the two advisors are compensated.  The first one is paid a percentage of the money you have invested with them.  The second one one is paid every time you purchase, sell, or move investments.  Which one do you choose?

My choice is advisor one, is that your choice as well?  I choose advisor one because his compensation incentives align with my goal of amassing as much money as possible before retirement.  If he wants to move my money from one investment to another I'm assuming because it will make me, and him, more money in the long run.

Now assume advisor two suggested moving your money from one investment to another.  Remember he gets paid everytime he buys or sells an investment on your behalf.  Is it in his best interest or yours to switch investments?  Even if he is honest and has of your best interest but there is still that nagging feeling in the back of your head because his compensation dose not align with your goals.  Does he need money for the fancy car he just bought?

Now lets switch the story from financial advisors to consultants.  How do you pay your consultants?  Most likely you pay them an hourly rate.  Does an hourly rate align with your project goals?

I would argue that in most cases it does not.  For example, say you want to reduce your support cost and decide a way best way to do this is to move information your customers usually call about to your website.  You find a consultant to do the work and he quotes you a hourly rate along with a estimate with how many hours it will take.

First note this hourly rate with estimate is really a quote.  Tell the truth, did you hear the hourly rate or did you just do the math and figure out the estimated total cost?  You probably just did this calculation:

SupportCostSavings - WebsiteEstimate = Profit

Second did you notice the consultant pushed the financial risk of the project from him to your company?  If the project goes over the estimate he still gets paid.  You could get rid of the consultant but then you need to find someone else and pay the cost to get them up to speed with the project.

From a financial point of view the consultant is motivated to use up all the estimated hours and, probably, exceed them.  There is no incentive for the consultant to deliver the project early.  No incentive the for the consultant to invest in tools or process that make them more efficient.  No incentive for the consultant to rein in changes.  In fact, more changes usually equals more hours, which equals more money for the consultant.

You will also start questing the consultants motives when he make recommendations, especially towards the end of the project when it's looking like it will go over the quote (hourly rate \* estimated hours).  Like financial advisor two the consultant is honest and has your best interests at heart but you still have that nagging feeling.

Now lets change the consultant from an hourly rate to a fixed price.  You don't pay more then the price quoted, even if the project runs late.  Now the consultant is motivated not to just get the project done one time but get it done it early.  It financially encourages him to be as efficient as possible including investing in tools and 3rd party software.  It encourage the consultant to rein in change requests or at least have a good conversation with you about them.
